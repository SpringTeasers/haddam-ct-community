Welcome to Haddam, Connecticut

One river, two banks, five villages — and a town that has been making its home on the Connecticut River since 1668.

Haddam sits in Middlesex County in the lower Connecticut River Valley, and it is the only town in Connecticut that the Connecticut River runs straight through rather than along its edge. Residents live on both banks: Higganum, Shailerville, Tylerville and Hidden Lake on the west side, and Haddam Neck on the east. With 8,452 people at the 2020 census across roughly 46 square miles, Haddam is a residential community with a working village center, a long shoreline, and a great deal of open space.

The river runs through the middle of it

Haddam holds a distinction no other Connecticut town can claim: the Connecticut River runs through the middle of the town, not along its border, so the town sits on both banks. For its first two hundred years the river was Haddam’s highway and its livelihood — shipyards were built along both sides, and the brooks feeding the river powered small mills and factories. Higganum Landing grew into a busy river port and shipbuilding center, and vessels built in local yards sailed as far as the West Indies.

The river still shapes daily life here, as a place to paddle, fish and walk, and as the reason the town’s villages each feel a little distinct.

Five villages, one town

Hidden Lake · Higganum · Shailerville · Tylerville · Haddam Neck

Because the river divides Haddam, the town grew as a set of distinct villages rather than one single center. Higganum, on the west bank, was first called “Hegamumpus” — “at axe rock” — and became the town’s industrial heart and a busy river port. Haddam Neck lies on the east bank. Haddam village is where the original English settlement was made.

Take part in town life

Haddam runs on volunteers and public meetings. Board and commission meetings are posted on the town calendar, and the town publishes news, alerts and a quarterly Haddam Events Magazine serving Haddam, Higganum and Haddam Neck.
